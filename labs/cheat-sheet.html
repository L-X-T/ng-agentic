The Cheat Sheet

One page. Eight modules. One invariant.

The Invariant every lab

Every piece of agentic work opens with a prompt and closes with your review and your approval – your commit in a live session; in an autonomous run, your commit of the run's diff or your merge of its branch. Workflows change what happens in between; autonomy changes how closely you watch – the bookends never move.

The Five-Part Prompt Lab 03 · 03-PROMPTING.md

  1. Observation – what you see: the bug, the smell, the missing behavior.
  2. Goal – the outcome, not the method.
  3. Constraints – what must not change.
  4. Scope – the files and folders; name paths.
  5. Check – a definition of done the agent can verify itself.

Levels: simple prompt → interactive ("plan and ask me questions, one at a time, before any code") → session: grilling (grill-me, grill-with-style) or brainstorming. Escalate with ambiguity, de-escalate when it's resolved.

Review – Close the Loop Lab 04 · 04-REVIEWING.md

  1. Machines first – build, lint, format, test; red output goes back to the agent.
  2. One explicit scope – current / staged / last commit / PR range.
  3. Two passes – correctness against requirements, then style-guide conformance.
  4. Independent reviewer – code-review skill, fresh context, ranked findings.
  5. Verify before acting – findings are claims; fix by severity, push back with evidence.
You own the final call – commit or merge. Always.

The contract before you leave: a verifiable goal + a feedback signal inside the run + guardrails (own branch/worktree, no push, no prod credentials). The invariant is deferred, not waived: review the accumulated diff.

The First Week After Lab 08 · 08-OUTLOOK.md

Promote one convention into your own AGENTS.md · port one skill · pick your first autonomous task (contract in hand) · wire one CI check an agent can use · decide your model & budget strategy.

Economics: compare configurations on the same acceptance cases; record review time, repairs and available usage/cost evidence. Distinguish enforced caps from alerts. Measure useful accepted outcomes, including correct diagnoses and justified rejections. Evaluate instructions before changing them.

Checks: verification contract. Choose authority, trigger, worker count, hosting and limits independently. Stop at verified success, a stated limit or an evidence-backed blocker. The live refactoring target is the small booking summary; the full legacy table is an extension.
